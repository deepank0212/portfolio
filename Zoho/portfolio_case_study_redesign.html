01

Context

This project is a conceptual UX audit and redesign of the Zoho CRM dashboard, focusing on improving the lead management experience for small businesses.

The primary user is Rahul, a small business owner and sales manager who manages Zoho CRM himself without a dedicated IT or CRM administrator. His goal is to quickly assign leads, monitor sales activity, and onboard his team with minimal training.

02

Problem

Rahul needs an interface that's simple to learn on his own. Cluttered navigation, poor discoverability, and inconsistent system feedback make common tasks — like assigning and tracking leads — harder than they should be, slowing onboarding and reducing productivity.

Recognition rather than recall

Dashboard widgets hide records below the fold with no indicator more exist, forcing extra scrolling to find them.

Visibility of system status

No confirmation appears after key actions like assigning or updating a lead — the user can't tell if it worked.

Consistency and standards

Widgets carry their own internal scrollbars while the page also scrolls — two competing scroll behaviors at once.

Recognition rather than recall

No recent-search history — users must recall exact record names instead of recognizing them from a list.

03

Process

I began by mapping the information architecture and user flow to simplify lead assignment for both the admin and sales rep. From there, I explored multiple wireframe directions, compared their trade-offs, and built out a consistent visual system — typography, colour, spacing, reusable components — before wiring an interactive prototype.

04

Iterations

Three directions were explored for the widget. Each solved the visibility problem differently — the trade-offs were about navigation cost and complexity, not just visual style.

Direction A — Rejected

View all, elsewhere

Capped rows with an explicit link — fixed the hidden-content problem, but still required leaving the widget entirely to see the rest.

Selected
Direction B

Expand in place

Same card grows to show everything. Solves the same visibility problem without breaking the user's context.

Direction C — Rejected

Urgency first

Surfaced only what needed attention today — required new backend logic to define "urgent," unjustified complexity for a simple widget.

Outcome

I tested the redesigned prototype with three users on common lead management tasks. All completed the tasks successfully, and none attempted to scroll horizontally or showed confusion about the new stacked layout.

06

Learnings

Good UX is about solving the right problem, not just improving the visual design. AI tools helped explore ideas quickly, but every suggestion needed evaluation against real user needs.

Instead of simply removing horizontal scrolling, I refined the solution to use a stacked layout on the dashboard while keeping full detail on a dedicated Leads page — progressive disclosure, not just deletion.

On overriding an AI suggestion

I also learned the importance of designing for accessibility, testing with real users, and documenting the reasoning behind every decision — not just the decision itself.
